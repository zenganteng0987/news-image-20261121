# THE DAILY ATLAS — Saturday 21 November 2026

A one-page picture of the world's news on Saturday 21 November 2026. The page carries 40 verified Saturday stories: 15 illustrated cards lead the layout — each with a painted scene and a source status label — a BY THE NUMBERS strip pulls out the day's key figures (GTA 6's $3.79bn day-one sales, the Nasdaq's −5.3% Friday close, the 100,000 UK–EU youth visas a year, the 370,000 people evacuated from Cyclone Akvan, and the 32.5m GTA 6 pre-orders), a THE DAY IN UTC timeline follows seven key moments of the day, and a DATE CHECK strip states that every story happened or was first reported on Saturday 21 November 2026. A dotted world map below carries a numbered pin for every story, so each item on the page can be located at a glance, next to a 25-item ALSO ON SATURDAY list. The day's biggest developments are all here: Russian missiles landing in south-east Poland, Wall Street sliding into correction, GTA 6 taking $3.79bn on day one, the UK–EU summit signing three deals, COP31 going into overtime with fossil-fuel language dropped, Cyclone Akvan weakening over Odisha, Anthropic shares sliding on day two, the Palisades fire defendant found guilty, Bayer's $7.25bn Roundup deal approved, Denmark foiling a Russian murder plot, Gupta jailed for 19 years 7 months in the Pannun plot, US sanctions on Iran's "oil guardians", the Senate passing an energy bill 62–33, India fighting back in the first Test, and an Anthropic researcher claiming a proof of the Riemann hypothesis — a claim, not yet independently verified. Every story was reported on the day itself and is drawn from wire and agency reporting — sources: Reuters, AP, BBC, CNN, The Hindu, Cricbuzz and others — and each story was confirmed against two independent sources before it earned its place on the page.

## How it works

The page itself (`entry.png`, 1024×1024) was composed programmatically with PIL. The entry program, `main.py`, simply publishes it: it checks that the bundled image is present and is a PNG, then copies `entry.png` to `/output/entry.png`, creating `/output` if needed, and verifies the copy matches the original by size and sha256. Running it repeatedly produces a byte-identical output. Python 3.13, standard library only, no network access.
